# Pilot treatment: "The First Conch"

**Series:** Mahabharat (working title), stylized 2D/3D hybrid animation.
**Pilot:** a 90 to 120 second proof-of-concept sequence, built through the full pipeline in `trailer-analysis/03-build-plan.md` before we scale to episodes.
**Source of record:** Ganguli, *Bhishma Parva* (`source/B06-bhishma.txt`). Line numbers below refer to that file. Section XXV starts at line 3432; Section XXXV starts at line 4805.

## Why this sequence

- It is the most recognizable moment in the epic: two armies, the conches, Arjuna's doubt and Krishna's universal form.
- It tests every element of the look: huge crowds and scale, a hero vehicle (the chariot), sacred light and geometry, a 2D sound-wave FX, impact frames, and a shift from a warm palette to a cosmic one.
- It needs only six characters at hero detail (Krishna, Arjuna, Bhishma, Duryodhana, Bhima, Yudhishthira). Everyone else can be crowd silhouettes.
- It is about 18 shots, which is small enough for one person with AI help to finish.

## Logline

On the morning of the great war, the conches of both armies sound across Kurukshetra. Arjuna asks Krishna to drive him between the lines, sees his teachers and kin in the enemy ranks, and lets his bow fall. Krishna answers by showing him what the universe really is.

## Tone and rules

- Dialogue is minimal: one or two lines from Arjuna (beats 6 and 8), quoted or close-paraphrased from Ganguli. Krishna's teaching is shown through the vision, not lectured.
- Sound design carries the sequence: wind, then a wall of conches, then silence, then a cosmic roar.
- Scale is the subject. Tiny figures, colossal sky.
- The palette moves from warm dawn (beats 1–5), to cold grey stillness (6–9), to cosmic gold and violet (10), and back to a hot war dawn (11–12). See `02-style-bible.md`.
