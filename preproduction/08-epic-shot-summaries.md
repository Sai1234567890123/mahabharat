# 08. What the epic says, shot by shot

Source: the Ganguli translation of the Bhishma Parva, `../mahabharat-site/source/B06-bhishma.txt` (line numbers below). Each entry is a summary in our own words of the lines the shot cites, followed by what the frame must show to be faithful. This is the reference every painted frame is checked against (`data/canon_checks.json`).

## Act A: Dawn of war

**SH010 The field at dawn** (2963-2964)
The two hosts stand arrayed. Above every standard on both sides towers one gigantic ape, on Arjuna's car. Thousands of foot soldiers with swords and spears march ahead to protect Bhima, and elephants follow.
*Show:* both hosts facing each other before battle; the ape standard on Arjuna's car, tallest on the field; nobody fighting yet.

**SH020 The ape banner** (2963-2964, 3212)
The ape on Arjuna's standard rises above all other standards. His car is called ape-bannered.
*Show:* the ape standard alone, dignified, above the field.

**SH030 The grandsire's roar** (3475-3478)
Duryodhana has just spoken to his commanders, telling them to guard Bhishma. To cheer him, Bhishma, the aged grandsire of the Kurus, gives a lion's roar.
*Show:* Bhishma, old and white-bearded, roaring; Duryodhana beside him, lifted by it.

**SH040 Bhishma's conch** (3476-3478)
Bhishma blows his conch. At once the Kaurava side sounds conches, drums, cymbals and horns, and the noise becomes a great uproar.
*Show:* Bhishma's conch blast first, then the Kaurava instruments; horns belong here, on the Kaurava side.

**SH050 Like a thousand suns** (3208-3213)
Arjuna's car has a hundred bells, is decked with the finest gold, has excellent wheels and shines like fire. White horses are yoked to it, and it looks as bright as a thousand suns. Krishna holds the reins of the ape-bannered car, and Arjuna stands in it with Gandiva and arrows, an archer without equal.
*Show:* many bells, gold, the car itself blazing, white horses, Krishna driving, Arjuna with bow and arrows.

**SH060 Panchajanya and Devadatta** (3478-3481)
Krishna and Arjuna, standing on their great car yoked to white horses, blow their celestial conches: Krishna blows Panchajanya, Arjuna blows Devadatta.
*Show:* both raising white conches, on the same car.

**SH070 They blow together** (3478-3481)
The same moment: the two conches sound together from the one car.
*Show:* both blowing at once; the ape standard on the car; no horns on this side.

**SH080 Paundra and Anantavijaya** (3481-3484)
Bhima, of terrible deeds, blows the huge conch Paundra. Yudhishthira blows Anantavijaya. Nakula and Sahadeva blow Sughosha and Manipushpaka.
*Show:* Bhima with a huge conch, Yudhishthira with his; the twins may appear.

**SH090 The blare** (3488-3490)
Then the kings and heroes of the Pandava side all blow their conches. The blare echoes through sky and earth and tears at the hearts of Dhritarashtra's sons and their army.
*Show:* the sound hitting the Kaurava ranks; soldiers shaken; the ground and sky trembling.

## Act B: Stillness

**SH100 Between the two armies** (3498-3508)
Just as missiles are about to fly, Arjuna raises his bow and asks Krishna to place the car between the two armies, so he can see the men he must fight.
*Show:* Arjuna lifting his bow and speaking to Krishna; the car starting forward.

**SH110 The empty ground** (3508-3510)
Krishna drives the car into the open ground between the hosts, in full view of Bhishma, Drona and all the kings, and says: behold these assembled Kurus.
*Show:* the car alone in the empty middle ground, with Bhishma and Drona visible ahead.

**SH120 Faces of kin** (3508-3514)
Arjuna sees, standing in both armies, fathers, grandfathers, teachers, uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, friends and in-laws. Seeing all his kinsmen, he is overcome by pity and speaks in despair.
*Show:* the faces of elders and teachers across the field (Bhishma, Drona) as Arjuna sees them.

**SH130 Gandiva slips** (3517-3521)
Arjuna tells Krishna that his limbs fail, his mouth is dry, his body shakes and his hair stands on end. Gandiva slips from his hand, his skin burns, he cannot stand, his mind wanders, and he sees bad omens. He wants neither victory nor kingdom.
*Show:* Arjuna sinking down, the bow slipping from his hand, Krishna still.

**SH140 Krishna turns** (Bhagavad Gita, Sections XXV-XLII, summarised)
Krishna answers Arjuna's grief with the teaching of the Gita: the soul is not slain, duty must be done without attachment to its fruits.
*Show:* Krishna turning to Arjuna, calm, about to speak.

## Act C: Vishvarupa

**SH150 The universal form** (4800-4935)
Arjuna asks to see Krishna's sovereign form. Krishna says his ordinary eyes cannot see it and gives him a divine eye. He then shows a form with:
- countless mouths, eyes, arms, thighs, feet and bellies, terrible with many tusks
- celestial ornaments, garlands and robes, and many celestial weapons raised; a diadem, mace and discus
- faces turned in every direction, with no beginning, middle or end
- the sun and moon for eyes and blazing fire for a mouth
- a radiance like a thousand suns bursting out at once, touching the sky and filling all space between heaven and earth
- the whole universe, divided into its many parts, held inside his body: all the gods, Brahma on his lotus, the sages and the celestial serpents

Hosts of gods enter him; some pray in fear with joined hands while sages and siddhas sing his praise. The three worlds tremble. Then Arjuna sees the sons of Dhritarashtra with their kings, and Bhishma, Drona and Karna, with warriors of the Pandava side too, rushing into those fanged, flaming mouths like rivers into the sea or moths into a flame. Some are caught between the teeth with their heads crushed. Krishna declares that he is Death, the destroyer of the worlds, that these warriors are already slain by him, and that Arjuna should be only his instrument.
*Show:* awe and terror, not calm. A form too vast to see whole, many fanged mouths, the cosmos and the gods inside the body, warriors (Bhishma, Drona, Karna among them) streaming into the fiery mouths, the sky on fire. Only Arjuna sees it, with the divine eye; the armies do not.

**SH160 Arjuna beholds** (4843-4862)
Arjuna is filled with amazement; his hair stands on end. He bows his head, joins his palms and speaks. He sees all the gods, Brahma on the lotus, the sages and the serpents inside the form, and can find no end, middle or beginning.
*Show:* Arjuna small, trembling, bowing with joined palms, face lit by the form.

## Act D: War dawn

**SH170 Arjuna rises** (5773)
At the end of the teaching, Arjuna says his delusion is destroyed and his memory of who he is has returned by Krishna's grace. He is firm, his doubts are gone, and he will do as Krishna says.
*Show:* Arjuna rising and lifting Gandiva; Krishna back at the reins.

**SH180 The banner, then black** (2963-2964, 3212)
The ape standard on Arjuna's car, above all others.
*Show:* the ape banner against the sun, then black.

## Why SH150 was wrong in every version so far

The text describes a **terrifying** form: countless fanged mouths, fire for a mouth, the universe and the gods inside the body, and the warriors of both armies, Bhishma, Drona and Karna by name, being swallowed. Krishna names himself Death, the destroyer of the worlds. Every SH150 frame painted so far shows a serene, benevolent, many-armed icon with a blessing hand in front of a yantra. That is the gentle devotional image, not this vision. Two smaller errors repeat as well: the armies are shown looking at the form, but only Arjuna is given the sight; and the cosmos-inside-the-body, the heart of the passage, is missing.
