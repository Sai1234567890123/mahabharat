# EP01 "The Anjalika": canon check (pipeline step 1)

Status: **draft, waiting for sai's sign-off** (2026-11-26).

Every shot A01 to A22 was checked against the Ganguli translation of the Karna Parva,
`mahabharat-research/source/B08-karna.txt`, lines 11555 to 12160. Line numbers below point into that file.
The same lines and verdict are stored in `shots.json` under each shot's `canon` field.

Verdicts:

- **canon**: the text describes it.
- **dramatised**: the text has the event, and we compress, stage or stylise it.
- **added**: not in the text. It is our framing, and it must not contradict the text.
- **changed**: the shot says something different from the text. sai has to choose to keep it or change it.

## Result in one paragraph

The order of events in EP01 matches Ganguli exactly: the net of arrows, then the serpent arrow and the crown, the sunken wheel, "Partha, wait", Krishna's rebuke, the banner, and finally the Anjalika and the light entering the sun. Nine shots are canon as written. Nine are fair dramatisations. Three are our own framing (A01, A03, A09) and contradict nothing. **One shot changes the text: A22.** In Ganguli, Krishna and Arjuna blow their conchs "filled with delight". Our Arjuna "does not celebrate". There are also two small fixes that need no change to any locked look: Shalya must be visible on Karna's car in the wide shots, and Karna's banner must carry the elephant's-rope device.

## Decisions for sai

1. **A22, the ending.** Choose one:
   - (a) **Keep "does not celebrate"** as a deliberate reading, and say so in the episode notes. This is the recommendation: the far conch already exists in the sound, and the stillness follows the "Show divinity through stillness" lesson in the research.
   - (b) **Follow the text.** Arjuna and Krishna raise Devadatta and Panchajanya and blow. The conch then comes from them, not from far away.
2. **Shalya in A02, A06 and A16.** He is in the Karna passport as "grey-bearded, mid-ground only", but no Shalya asset exists. The proposal is a simple back-turned or silhouetted charioteer made by reusing the Krishna body under a new paint. This is free and done in Blender.

## Period-accuracy checklist (use on every frame before approval)

Look at every layout grab and render against this list. A "no" on any line means the shot is not approved.

- [ ] **Screen direction:** Arjuna and Krishna on the left, facing right; Karna and Shalya on the right; the sun behind Karna (bible).
- [ ] **Charioteers:** Krishna drives Arjuna. Shalya drives Karna and is visible in every wide shot of Karna's car.
- [ ] **Banners:** the ape (Hanuman) on Arjuna's flagstaff; the elephant's rope on Karna's, gold and jewelled. No modern flags, no text, no symbols from later centuries.
- [ ] **Horses:** Arjuna's are white with gold trappings and four to a car. Karna's stay dun, by the passport choice (canon says white; this was kept on purpose so the teams read apart).
- [ ] **Chariots:** two spoked wheels, a wooden car with gold work, bells and banners. No suspension, no steel bolts or rivet rows, nothing that reads as machined metal.
- [ ] **Weapons:** bows, arrows and quivers only in these shots. Gandiva has black limbs with gold bands; Vijaya is Karna's. No swords drawn, no shields on Arjuna, no crossbows.
- [ ] **Costume:** Karna has no earrings and no natural armour (gone before the war). Arjuna wears the diadem until A14 and is bare-headed from A14 on, with no cloth band. No stitched trousers, no buttons, no zips, no modern shoes.
- [ ] **Conches:** Devadatta is Arjuna's, Panchajanya is Krishna's. Only conchs, drums and battle cries; no brass bugles or trumpets in the picture.
- [ ] **Time of day:** afternoon into a darkening sun. The death is "in the afternoon" (12118), not at night.
- [ ] **Ground and world:** red earth, dust, broken weapons. No paved roads, buildings, glass, plastic, electric light, or tyre tracks.
- [ ] **Never-list:** no gore, no severed head on screen, no real-actor likeness, no text in frame.
- [ ] **Sources:** nothing in the frame was traced or generated from film, TV or another artist's work.
