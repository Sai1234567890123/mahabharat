# Baahubali: The Eternal War teaser: shot and style analysis

Reference study for the Mahabharat AI series. Written from 367 frames that sai captured from the Telugu teaser (runtime 2:27). Every shot is described in words; no frame is copied, re-exported or embedded here, so this document still works after the screenshots are deleted.

## How to read this

- Frame numbers (F1 to F367) are the screenshots in filename order. They are kept only so the order is traceable while the folder still exists.
- Timecodes come from the player's progress bar where it was readable, so treat them as approximate (about ±2 seconds). The frames cover 0:00 to about 2:11; the last ~16 seconds (most likely the title and credits) were not captured, and there is a gap between about 1:31 and 1:40.
- Two files are not real frames: F310 is a 6×7 pixel blank and F325 is a 188×88 sliver. A few frames show the YouTube player UI, a pause icon or a desktop taskbar; these were ignored.
- Abbreviations: ECU extreme close-up, CU close-up, MCU medium close-up, MS medium shot, WS wide shot, EWS extreme wide, OTS over the shoulder, DOF depth of field.

## 3. Camera language

- **Slow push-ins on faces** are the default for character beats (shots 14, 15, 55, 65, 69, 76, 82). They are slow and steady, never handheld.
- **Locked-off, centered symmetry** is reserved for ritual: the dance at the pillar (12, 32, 62), the multi-armed reveal (78). The stillness makes these feel sacred.
- **Extreme wides with tiny figures** sell scale: the cliff (13), the falling wall (34), the statue head and hand (44, 45), the standoff (68), the chariot (75). Characters often take up under a tenth of the frame.
- **Physically impossible cameras** in action: the camera free-falls and tumbles with the fighters (35), sits on the floor under a giant foot (42), looks straight down on a mid-air duel (48), and dives past pillars (50). Dutch angles of 30 to 60 degrees are common.
- **Whip pans and smears** link action cuts; motion blur is exaggerated and often painted rather than physically correct.
- **Rack focus and foreground framing** (17, 39, 55) add depth: a hand, weapon or shoulder close to the lens with action beyond.
- **Lens character**: anamorphic horizontal flares, halation around light sources, chromatic aberration at frame edges, lens bulge on impacts. The virtual camera is treated like a real, imperfect lens.

## 4. Editing and transitions

- **Impact frames** are the signature device: 1 to 3 frames of flat graphic imagery (black silhouette on red, white on black, ink splats, inverted negative) cut into a hit. Count in the captured frames: about 20 of them.
- **Negative and inverted flashes** (F4, F8, F296, F303, F318) work like a cut and a strobe at the same time.
- **Whiteouts** (F77, F342) bridge explosions to the next scene.
- **Graphic wipes**: painted energy (F205–207, F267–268), dust walls (F78–79) and line-art mandalas (F17–18) fill the frame and become the transition.
- **Same beat, many renderings** (F225–229): one pose shown as 3D, as a comic drawing and as a two-tone print in quick succession.
- **Rhythm**: action blocks run 10 to 15 seconds of fast cutting (many shots under 1 second), then a calm ritual interlude of 2 to 4 seconds of locked camera.
- **Text**: one lower-right, two-line place card early on (a small descriptor line over a larger place name). No other on-screen text in the captured range.

## 5. How 2D and 3D are combined

What the frames show, layer by layer:

1. **3D foundation.** Characters, sets, the chariot, elephants, statues and floating ruins are 3D models with real cameras and lighting. Staging, perspective and camera moves are fully 3D (orbiting, falling, top-down cameras would be very hard in pure 2D).
2. **Painted surface.** Skin, cloth, stone and metal have hand-painted textures with visible brush texture and flat value blocks rather than photoreal detail. Shadows have hard-ish edges; rim light is strong and colored (magenta, cyan). Hair is grouped into painted clumps. This is the Arcane-style look the press compares it to.
3. **Painted backgrounds.** Skies, clouds and far landscapes read as matte paintings: loose brushwork, flat color fields, atmospheric haze.
4. **2D effects animation.** Fire, lightning, energy beams, sparks, speed lines, shield mandalas, smoke puffs and impact smears are hand-drawn or painted. They are often hard-edged, 2 to 3 tones, and appear to run on fewer frames ("on twos") than the camera and characters. Smoke and dust are cel-shaded puffs, not realistic volumes.
5. **Line-art graphics in 3D space.** Mandalas, the Sri Yantra, lotus shields and the Shiva face are glowing vector-like line work placed into the 3D scene (they sit on planes or spheres, perspective-correct).
6. **Fully 2D inserts.** The impact frames, the brushstroke fight (F67–68) and the comic-panel and screen-print versions of the warrior (F226, F228–229) are full-frame 2D illustrations cut between 3D shots. Press reports also say a separate hand-drawn sequence is being made by Studio Khara for the film.
7. **Compositing and lens.** Chromatic aberration, bloom, flares, grain and grade tie everything together.

Rule of thumb from the trailer: **3D owns form, space and camera; 2D owns energy, texture and emotion.**

## 6. Lighting and color

- **Overall palette**: a saturated pink-red world (Paathala Lokam) for most of the trailer; dark violet-black interiors for the shrine; a shift to storm grey-blue for the finale so the hero's gold and the warrior's blue pop.
- **Character color coding**: the demon is red skin with gold, green and black; the deva-warrior is blue skin with gold armor; the hero is warm brown skin with ash-white marks and red-orange cloth. Their energy colors follow: demon gets orange sparks and green blasts, the warrior gets cyan lightning and white-violet shields, the hero gets turquoise-magenta ritual fire and orange-gold arrows.
- **Key light** is usually a hot backlight or sun low in frame (sunsets, god rays, halos), with the face lit by colored fill. Faces are often half in shadow.
- **Atmosphere**: heavy haze and depth fog in every wide; volumetric shafts in the shrine.
- **Contrast**: impact frames reduce everything to 2 or 3 colors (red, black, white, sometimes cyan), the strongest possible contrast with the painterly shots around them.
- **Selective color**: background drained to grayscale while the character stays red (shot 21).

## 7. World-building cues

- **Afterlife cosmology**: a soul leaves the body (shot 6) and enters through a portal resolved as a Sri Yantra; the press describes the film as a journey through the 14 realms (lokas) of Indian cosmology. The place card names Paathala Lokam (the underworld) and the fallen city of Tripura (the triple city of the asuras).
- **Shattered scale**: the world is broken into floating islands, tilted fortresses, sideways temple corridors and colossal fragments of deity statues (a helmeted head, a hand in a mudra). Gravity is unreliable, which justifies the free-fall fights.
- **Sacred architecture**: a giant lingam-like pillar with three lines and a red dot, carved columns, rings of broken pillars, war-elephant statues, ornate gateways.
- **Divine technology**: a flying golden chariot drawn by armored elephants with gem crests and a royal parasol (a vimana), force fields shaped as mandalas and yantras.
- **Iconography**: tripundra ash lines, V-shaped tilak, bindi, rudraksha beads, mudras, the Nataraja pose, a multi-armed divine form, the third eye.
- **Performance culture**: the dance draws on Indian classical forms; the press says the choreography uses Kalaripayattu, Chhau and Kuchipudi.

## 8. Character design notes

- **Hero**: a stylized likeness of the live-action actor; big hair, heavy beard, ash marks, rudraksha mala, scars and blood. Painted realism is pushed furthest on him.
- **Demon king**: hulking proportions, red skin, enormous beard, tall gold crown, rings and armlets with large gems, chained mace. He gets the most screen time and the most animation acting (grins, snarls, glowing eyes, blood tears).
- **Deva-warrior**: blue skin, slim heroic build, tall crown with a sun-ray halo, gold breastplate with a lotus emblem, flowing cloth; becomes multi-armed. Lightning is his visual signature.
- **Shape language**: exaggerated silhouettes (huge torso and small head for the demon; long limbs and tall crown for the warrior) so each reads instantly even as a black silhouette in an impact frame.

## 9. Style rules to carry into the Mahabharat series

1. Build every shot in 3D for space and camera; paint the surface; draw the energy.
2. Give each major character a skin color, accent color and energy color, and keep them consistent in every shot and every impact frame.
3. Design silhouettes that read as pure black shapes.
4. Use locked, symmetrical frames for the sacred; use free, tumbling cameras for battle.
5. Cut 1 to 3 frame graphic impact frames into every big hit.
6. Sell scale with tiny figures against colossal broken statuary and architecture.
7. Use sacred geometry (yantra, mandala, lotus) as both magic FX and transitions.
8. Keep one recurring ritual image that the edit returns to as a rhyme (for Mahabharat: Krishna's conch, the chariot at Kurukshetra, Draupadi's fire).
9. Treat the lens as imperfect: flares, color fringing, bloom, grain.
10. Shift the whole palette per act so each act has its own color identity.
